A clearer path for high-impact projects

Good projects should not get lost in the process.

Ventura's Catalyst Projects Initiative is a proposed, coordinated way to help significant development, business expansion, redevelopment, and investment projects move through City Hall with clarity—without skipping public review or community priorities.

Make the City easier to navigate—while keeping the public process intact.

The initiative responds to a simple tension: valuable projects need speed and certainty, while Ventura needs review, transparency, and accountability.

01 / CLARITY

One front door

A designated City contact helps project teams understand who is involved, what information is needed, and where to go next.

02 / COORDINATION

One shared path

Departments coordinate key reviews, surface conflicts earlier, and use milestones that make progress visible to everyone.

03 / TRUST

One accountable record

Projects receive clearer expectations—not a free pass. Public requirements, decision authority, and community input remain visible.

The guardrails

Faster does not mean less careful.

01

Same rules, better coordination

Expedited coordination can reduce avoidable delay without waiving environmental, safety, zoning, building, or public-hearing requirements.

02

Clear eligibility, not special favors

Projects should qualify based on published criteria such as investment, jobs, redevelopment, strategic fit, or meaningful community benefit.

03

Milestones are targets, not promises to approve

The City can commit to communication, coordination, and predictable handoffs while preserving the authority of each review and decision-maker.

04

Measure what improves

Ventura should track time, rework, project outcomes, and participant experience—then adjust the framework based on evidence.

Who is the Catalyst lane for?

Catalyst support is meant for projects with the potential to create substantial public value—not only for one preferred industry.

QUALIFYING SIGNAL 01

Investment & jobs

New or expanded activity that brings meaningful private investment, quality jobs, or a stronger local economic base.

QUALIFYING SIGNAL 02

Place-based revitalization

Redevelopment or adaptive reuse that helps activate an underused site, corridor, district, or strategic area.

QUALIFYING SIGNAL 03

Community benefit

A project that advances public priorities through services, amenities, infrastructure, housing, sustainability, or other measurable benefits.

QUALIFYING SIGNAL 04

Strategic fit

Projects may connect to Ventura's business strengths—from healthcare and technology to advanced manufacturing, the port, and the blue economy.

A practical path from first conversation to measurable result.

The first version can launch as a lightweight playbook, using existing staff coordination before new technology or major funding is required.

0–3 MONTHS

Screen & scope

Receive a project intake, test eligibility, name a lead contact, and identify the departments and outside partners involved.

Output: Catalyst project brief
3–6 MONTHS

Align the route

Hold a cross-department kickoff, create a review map, identify information gaps, and set target milestones.

Output: shared action plan
6–12 MONTHS

Coordinate review

Maintain regular check-ins, track handoffs, communicate decisions and next steps, and escalate blockers when needed.

Output: visible status record
12+ MONTHS

Evaluate & improve

Compare baseline and Catalyst projects, gather feedback, and recommend what should become a standard City practice.

Output: annual performance readout
The day-to-day playbook

Make the concierge model work for development.

Ventura has already used a single point of contact to coordinate special-event and film permitting. Catalyst can adapt that proven idea for complex development projects.

01

Start with a feasibility meeting

Before formal submission, the project team meets with the coordinator and relevant departments to identify site, infrastructure, entitlement, and community questions.

02

Use the four-part staff rubric

Review projects for catalytic impact, strategic alignment, catalyst need, and readiness. Use the rubric to support consistent judgment—not to force unlike projects into a false ranking.

03

Coordinate, do not bypass

The coordinator maintains a shared action plan, makes existing expedited review options visible, and escalates blockers while preserving every required review and public decision.

04

Track promises after approval

Follow project-specific commitments over time—jobs, investment, infrastructure, local business participation, or other stated benefits—so residents can see whether the project delivered.

Questions people may have.

A clear program should make expectations easier to understand for project teams, residents, and City staff.

Does Catalyst status guarantee approval?

No. Catalyst status would improve coordination and communication; it would not change the City's legal requirements, decision authority, or public process.

Does every project in a priority industry qualify?

No. Industry is one possible signal, not an automatic qualification. The program should stay flexible and focus on measurable economic or community benefit.

What happens if a project gets stuck?

The lead contact documents the blocker, brings the right departments together, and escalates unresolved issues through a defined internal path—without promising an outcome that the City cannot control.

How could Ventura start without a large new budget?

Begin with a published intake, a staff playbook, a named coordinator, recurring interdepartmental check-ins, and a simple status tracker. Technology and incentives can be added after the pilot proves where they create value.

The opportunity

Build a process that helps Ventura say “let’s figure this out.”

The Catalyst Projects Initiative gives the City a practical starting point for turning high-potential projects into coordinated, accountable action.
